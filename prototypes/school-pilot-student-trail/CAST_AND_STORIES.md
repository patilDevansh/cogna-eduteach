# The Area cast: seven people, seven real problems

Design for the seven Area lessons (trail Days 3–9) and the Rive Area module. Written 27 Nov 2026, before building.
It replaces the farm world (Ramesh uncle's field, Lakshmi aunty, Meena, canals and fences) everywhere.

## Why the old stories failed

- **One world, seven times.** Every lesson was a field next to a canal. By Day 6 a student has stopped looking.
- **Costume shorthand.** "Uncle" was a turban and a moustache, "aunty" was a bun. That is how a cartoon spots a
  type, not how anyone looks.
- **The adult needs rescuing.** A farmer can't work out his own field until a 13-year-old does it for him. Real
  adults who work with land, cloth or tiles are the experts in their own work.
- **The same idea, taught twice a day.** Day 3 taught "fence or land" as a story *and* as a Rive lesson, and the
  wonky field twice too. Repetition was built into the day, not only into the voice.

## Rules for every story

1. **A young person leads, with a goal of their own.** Each story starts with something a 13-year-old wants: to
   win a street contest, finish a skate ramp, ship a game level. The maths is the tool that gets it done.
2. **Adults are experts, never people to be rescued.** The aunt *is* the solar engineer; the mason *is* the one who
   knows tiles. The student works alongside them.
3. **Real places, real details.** A named city, and one true everyday detail (rice-flour kolam on a dot grid,
   Ladakhi solar greenhouses, women masons in Jharkhand, Ahmedabad's kite workshops before Uttarayan). Nothing
   invented about a community.
4. **No costumes.** Everyday clothes: jerseys, hoodies, T-shirts, school uniform, a work saree with a helmet, a
   warm jacket in Leh. Many skin tones, hair types and body types. Glasses, a hearing aid, a wheelchair, shown as
   part of life and never as the problem to solve.
5. **Names that belong together.** First names and surnames that people from that place really have.
6. **One greeting, not a lesson about culture.** Each person says hello their own way ("Vanakkam", "Juley",
   "Chibai", "Johar", "Kem cho") and then gets on with it.
7. **Balance.** Four girls and three boys; North, South, East, West, Central and North-East India; cities, towns
   and a village; families of different faiths and Adivasi families; nobody is a farmer.
8. **The student is a crew member, not a saviour.** "You" help each friend with one problem. On Day 10 they all
   appear together on the check-in screen.

## Each lesson: what the student does

Every lesson keeps the agreed recipe (story → guess → hands → tell → rule) and the existing Rive mechanics that
already work, with the farm swapped for the real setting. **Gold** still means "this moves, or this is where it
goes". Everything that can be dragged has a keyboard path.

**Day 3 · Kavin's kolam patch (Rive lessons 1–3, one step).**
1. *Dot grid.* Paati's patch is a grid of dots. Drag across it to sprinkle rice-flour squares; they fall, settle
   and tick. Tap rows to count them: 6, 12, 18, 24 → 6 × 4.
2. *The rope.* Every house gets 20 metres of rope. **Bet first**: if the rope stays the same, can the space inside
   change? Drag a corner; a live chart plots every patch. Find the biggest (5 × 5 = 25), then squeeze the same rope
   into the smallest (9 × 1 = 9). The rope is the perimeter; the floor is the area.
3. *The corner house.* The street bends, so the last patch leans. Swipe a straight cut, carry the piece across:
   it clicks in as a rectangle with the same squares.

**Day 4 · Tenzin's greenhouse wall (Rive lesson 4).**
1. Tap the triangular end wall for a copy, spin it with its gold knob, slide it in: a parallelogram, then half.
2. Drag the roof's top corner along the ridge line: the slant changes, the sheet needed does not.
3. *The plumb line.* Dolkar hangs a stone on a string from the top. Drag it along the base until it hangs
   straight; tap the real height. The slanted roof beam is the trap.
4. Keypad: base 10, height 4, slanted side 5 → 20.

**Day 5 · Remi's pennants (practice day, no new lesson).** Questions and the worked example use Remi's pennants
and banners: pairs of triangles that make a rectangle.

**Day 6 · Kiran's funbox (Rive lesson 5).** A skater rolls over the ramp as the lesson opens.
1. Tap the side panel for a copy, spin it, slide it in: a parallelogram with base 5 + 3 = 8.
2. Keypad: the whole parallelogram (32), then half (16).
3. Stretch the ramp's flat top with a gold handle: shrink it to a point (a triangle) or match the bottom (a
   parallelogram). One rule covers them all.
4. Keypad: top 4, bottom 8, height 5 → 30.

**Day 7 · Imran's island (Rive lesson 6).** Pixel-art islands on a tile grid; coins pop on as tiles are counted.
1. Drag along a grid line to split the L-shaped island into rectangles; each lights up with its own count.
2. Split it the other way: the same total.
3. Drag a gold block into the empty corner to make one big rectangle, then take it away: 48 − 12.
4. Keypad: a U-shaped island → 26.

**Day 8 · Birsa and Phulmani Oraon's kitchen floor (Rive lesson 7).**
1. One square metre of floor. Zoom with the slider until centimetres appear: 100 × 100 = 10,000.
2. Drag a corner to scale a square: twice as wide holds 4 copies, three times holds 9.
3. Phulmani Oraon's question: how many square centimetres is one square metre tile? → 10,000.

**Day 9 · Fiza's kites (new Rive lesson 8).**
1. A kite is two triangles. Tap a triangle's real height; for a normal triangle it is inside.
2. Nani's leaning triangle: the height won't fit inside. Drag the base line longer, then drop the plumb line
   outside the shape. The height is still straight up from the base line.
3. Keypad: a leaning kite triangle, base 6, height 4 (outside) → 12.

## How the Rive lessons join the trail

- **Opened directly.** The trail sets the lesson number; the student never lands on the lesson list.
- **One voice.** The lesson does not play its own recordings. It hands each line to the page, and the trail's voice
  player says it (recorded line, else the fallback voice). The voice stops the moment the student moves on.
- **Evidence.** Each keypad answer is reported to the trail (right or wrong, with the mix-up it shows) and recorded
  like any other answer. "Lesson done" unlocks Continue.
