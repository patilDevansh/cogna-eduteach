# Cogna — home page brief

Everything a designer needs to build the Cogna home page, in plain English. Facts come from `docs/mvp-10.0/README.md`, `COGNA/HOW_THE_PRODUCT_WORKS.md`, and the working product.

**The page is about one thing: personalised learning.** Every student gets their own test, their own lesson, their own practice, and their own next step. Everything else on this page exists to make that believable.

---

## 1. What Cogna is, in one line

Cogna replaces one-size-fits-all maths teaching with a lesson built around how each student actually thinks.

Longer version: Cogna is a personalised maths tutor for Grade 8 CBSE students in India. It learns exactly where one student's algebra breaks, then builds that student's own lesson, their own practice, and their own follow-up check around that one gap. Two students in the same class get two different tests, two different videos, and two different next steps.

---

## 2. The problem it solves

A class of 40 gets one lesson, one worksheet, and one test. The teacher has no way to know that Aarav's problem is signs, Meena's is what "factorised" even means, and Rohan just stops halfway. So all three get the same revision — which fits none of them.

Cogna gives each of those three students a different test, a different explanation, and a different next step, and gives the teacher one page saying who needs what.

---

## 3. What is personalised, exactly (the heart of the page)

Say these concretely. This is what competitors can't show.

| For each student | What's personalised |
|---|---|
| **The test** | Their own 25 questions, written for them, in the moment. Not a fixed paper. |
| **While they're testing** | The questions change as they answer. A mistake brings a different question on the same skill later. A confirmed gap removes what depends on it and drops down to the basics underneath. |
| **The lesson** | A video made for that student's own gap, narrated — not a library video about the chapter. |
| **The practice** | Challenges aimed at their mistake, not the chapter average. |
| **The proof** | A fresh check built from their own evidenced gap, with no help, to see if it's actually fixed. |
| **What home hears** | A letter about that child: what happened, what it might mean, what to do next. |

**A concrete example to put on the page** (real, from a live run):

> Aarav wrote `−6(−x − 3)`. He'd taken out the −6 but kept the signs inside. The test didn't move on; it came back later with a different negative-factor question. He did the same thing again — so the gap was confirmed, not guessed. His next questions were rebuilt around it, and one question that needed the broken skill was replaced by an easier one testing the same idea another way. His lesson was about exactly that.
>
> Meena, same class, same day: her problem was thinking `2y(x + 1) + 3(x + 1)` was already factorised. Different gap, different test, different lesson.

## 4. How it works (four stages, teacher-driven)

1. **Class** — The teacher makes a class and gets a join code. Students join on their own devices.
2. **Find the gap** — The teacher launches the diagnostic for everyone. Each student takes their own adaptive test, up to 25 questions.
3. **Teach it** — Once everyone's done, personalised teaching goes out: each student's own video and practice.
4. **Prove it** — After teaching, a fresh independent check with no hints. Only this counts as proof it worked.

The teacher sees one report: who has which gap, and afterwards, who can now do it alone.

---

## 5. Why this personalisation can be trusted

Personalised is easy to claim. These are the reasons ours is real:

- **The maths is checked by code, not AI opinion.** Every answer is marked by exact algebra. AI reads the student's working to explain *why* it went wrong, but can never change right to wrong.
- **Personal doesn't mean slow.** The next question appears the instant the student submits. Their questions are written and checked in the background, before they get there.
- **One mistake is never a verdict.** A gap is only called a gap when the same skill fails twice, on two different questions.
- **It says what it doesn't know.** Questions that got removed are reported as "not tested", never as wrong.
- **It never labels a child.** No IQ, no ADHD, no personality, no "weak student". Only what was observed, on which step.

---

## 6. Honest limits (design them in, don't hide them)

- A working prototype in pilot, not a validated school product.
- Covers Grade 8 algebra today: linear equations with brackets, and factorisation.
- The diagnostic is labelled experimental inside the product.
- A watched video or a game score is practice, never proof of mastery.

**Never claim:** exam-score improvements, learning styles, "AI understands your child", any clinical label, or that this replaces the teacher. Cogna hands the teacher better information; it doesn't take the class.

---

## 7. Voice

- Calm, plain, specific. Short sentences.
- Say the exact thing: "Aarav's problem is signs; Meena's is what factorised means" beats "tailored learning journeys".
- No hype words: revolutionary, cutting-edge, seamless, unlock, empower, 10x.
- Avoid the empty version of our own claim. Don't write "deeply personalised learning experience"; show two students getting two different things.
- Indian school context: CBSE, Grade 8, teacher-first. Example names: Aarav, Meena, Rohan, Aadya.

---

## 8. Brand cues already in use (keep or evolve)

- **Current hero line:** "Find the exact step where algebra breaks." Keep the precision, but the new hero should land on *personalised*: the exact step is how we personalise, not the end goal.
- **The worked line motif:** a real equation solving itself — `3(x + 4) = 21 → 3x + 12 = 21 → 3x = 9 → x = 3`.
- **"Mistake Microscope":** an interactive block showing the student's wrong line beside the correct one, with the exact step highlighted.
- **Reports read as letters**, not dashboards.
- Three sign-ins: student, parent, teacher.
- Current look: light, airy, plenty of white space, one accent colour, real maths in a proper maths face.

---

## 9. Suggested page structure

1. **Hero** — one lesson doesn't fit 40 students. Cogna builds one per student. Three sign-in buttons.
2. **Two students, side by side** — the strongest block on the page. Aarav and Meena, same class, same chapter: different mistake, different question next, different lesson. Show it, don't describe it.
3. **What's personalised** — the test, the lesson, the practice, the proof, the letter home.
4. **How it works** — the four stages.
5. **Why you can trust it** — code-checked maths, two mistakes before we call it a gap, "not tested" is a real answer, no labels.
6. **For teachers / for parents / for students** — three short blocks, each ending in the right sign-in.
7. **Honest band** — what stage the product is at and what it covers today.
8. **Footer** — Cogna · Grade 8 algebra · pilot.
